## Abstract

Popular low-rank parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods represent adaptation as separate updates to selected backbone weights, without maintaining an explicit task-specific state that is updated and reused across depth. These updates also require the backbone at inference and therefore cannot operate as standalone predictors. We propose ShadowPEFT, which consolidates trainable adaptation into a modular shadow component centered on a compact shadow model and lightweight Transformer layer-specific coupling modules. A persistent shadow state refines the frozen backbone representations and is updated from them in an interactive manner. Because the shadow model is trained as a complete predictor, it can be detached for shadow-only inference without executing the base model and can be initialized from a pretrained model. Experiments on text and image generation and understanding benchmarks show that ShadowPEFT matches or outperforms LoRA and DoRA under comparable trainable-parameter budgets. Additional analyses on shadow pretraining, cross-dataset transfer, parameter scaling, inference latency, and system-level evaluation suggest that centralized layer-space adaptation is a competitive and flexible alternative to conventional low-rank PEFT.

## Contributions (from the paper)

We evaluate ShadowPEFT on six text and image generation and understanding benchmarks, including MMLU (text) (Hendrycks et al., 2021), GSM8K (text) (Cobbe et al., 2021), SQuADv2 (text) (Rajpurkar et al., 2018), DreamBooth (image) (Ruiz et al., 2023), Amazon Review (text) (Keung et al., 2020), and 20 Newsgroups (text) (Mitchell, 1997). Across Qwen backbones at three scales, compact ShadowPEFT is competitive with LoRA and DoRA under closely matched trainable-parameter budgets, while enabling modular deployment modes that are not naturally supported by conventional low-rank PEFT. Our contributions are as follows: 1. We introduce ShadowPEFT, a modular representation-space PEFT framework that organizes adaptation around a persistent shadow state and couples it to a frozen backbone through shadow-to-base injection and base-to-shadow gated updates. 2. We provide a detachable shadow model that supports both attached refinement and shadow-only inference, enabling deployment patterns such as edge–cloud collaboration while separating architectural availability from standalone predictive quality. 3. Across multiple benchmarks and backbone scales, ShadowPEFT achieves competitive performance relative to LoRA and DoRA under comparable trainable-parameter budgets, while demonstrating stronger out-of-distribution transfer in our cross-dataset evaluation.

## Conclusion (from the paper)

We have presented ShadowPEFT, a modular PEFT framework that introduces an explicit depth-wise state for coordinating task adaptation across layers. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ShadowPEFT achieves competitive performance against LoRA and DoRA under comparable trainable-parameter budgets. Beyond raw accuracy, the framework offers unique deployment advantages: the shadow model can be detached and used independently for inference, supporting edge–cloud collaboration and other resource-constrained scenarios. Despite these benefits, ShadowPEFT introduces additional computational overhead during training due to the forward pass through both the backbone and the shadow model. Future work may explore more efficient shadow architectures. We believe ShadowPEFT opens a promising direction for rethinking how task-specific knowledge is structured and propagated in large-scale model adaptation.

## Limitations (from the paper)

We have identified several limitations from current model design. First, unlike LoRA and its variants, whose trained weights can be easily merged with those of the base model, ShadowPEFT’s shadow model cannot be merged into the base model directly, which may complicate deployment in settings that require a single unified checkpoint. Second, the dual-path design of ShadowPEFT incurs a modest increase in peak GPU memory usage due to the dual KV-cache. While this trade-off preserves high-speed token generation and remains fully compatible with standard KV-caching optimizations, it could be a concern for extremely resource-constrained environments. Future work will explore more efficient architectural designs to reduce the memory overhead and investigate alternative strategies for merging or distilling the shadow model into the base model.
