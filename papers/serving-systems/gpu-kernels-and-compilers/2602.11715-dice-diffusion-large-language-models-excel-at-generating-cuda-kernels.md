## Abstract

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) have emerged as a compelling alternative to autoregressive (AR) LLMs, owing to their capacity for parallel token generation. This paradigm is particularly well-suited for code generation, where holistic structural planning and non-sequential refinement are critical. Despite this potential, tailoring dLLMs for CUDA kernel generation remains challenging, obstructed not only by the high specialization but also by the severe lack of high-quality training data. To address these challenges, we construct CuKe, an augmented supervised fine-tuning dataset optimized for high-performance CUDA kernels. On top of it, we propose a bi-phase curated reinforcement learning (BiC-RL) framework consisting of a CUDA kernel infilling stage and an end-to-end CUDA kernel generation stage. Leveraging this training framework, we introduce DICE, a series of diffusion large language models designed for CUDA kernel generation, spanning three parameter scales, 1.7B, 4B, and 8B. Extensive experiments on KernelBench demonstrate that DICE significantly outperforms both autoregressive and diffusion LLMs of comparable scale, establishing a new state-of-the-art for CUDA kernel generation.

## Contributions (from the paper)

Motivated by these observations, we introduce DICE, a family of diffusion large language models tailored for CUDA kernel generation, spanning parameter sizes of 1.7B, 4B, and 8B. Rather than treating diffusion as a drop-in replacement for autoregression, DICE is designed to exploit the specific strengths of dLLMs for this task through task-specific supervision and reinforcement learning. Our key contributions are summarized as follows: ① We introduce CuKe, a specifically curated supervised fine-tuning dataset that contains a large number of high-performance CUDA kernels; ② We propose BiC-RL, a new reinforcement learning paradigm for the CUDA kernel generation task that features a hierarchical progression from easy to hard across both the CUDA kernel data and task complexity; ③ Leveraging BiC-RL, we present DICE, which is the first specialized dLLM designed for CUDA kernel generation, to our best knowledge; ④ Extensive experiment results demonstrate that DICE achieves the state-of-the-art performance against both autoregressive and diffusion LLMs.

## Conclusion (from the paper)

In this paper, we curate the CuKe dataset to provide a robust foundation for supervised fine-tuning, mitigating the scarcity of high-quality training data in this domain. Then we introduce BiC-RL, which is specifically designed for CUDA kernel generation. Combining kernel infilling and end-to-end kernel generation stages can effectively improve training efficiency and enhance convergence. Our extensive experiments demonstrate that each training stage contributes significantly to stable convergence, and their combination yields substantial performance improvements. By integrating these components, we develop DICE, a family of diffusion large language models spanning three parameter scales, opening up a new perspective for this specialized area.

## Limitations (from the paper)

The current BiC-RL framework primarily adopts an execution-based binary reward signal. While this ensures the model can generate functionally correct CUDA kernels, a prerequisite for any performance optimization, it does not explicitly incorporate execution latency into the reward function. Given the high variance and environmental noise associated with measuring the speedup of CUDA kernels in a distributed training setting, we prioritize the stability of the reinforcement learning process. We consider the integration of multi-objective rewards as an important direction for future research.
