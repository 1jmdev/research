## Abstract

We develop a principled method for quantization-aware training (QAT) of large-scale machine learning models. Specifically, we show that convex, piecewise-affine regularization (PAR) can effectively induce the model parameters to cluster towards discrete values. We minimize PAR-regularized loss functions using an aggregate proximal stochastic gradient method (AProx) and prove that it has last-iterate convergence. Our approach provides an interpretation of the straight-through estimator (STE), a widely used heuristic for QAT, as the asymptotic form of PARQ. We conduct experiments to demonstrate that PARQ obtains competitive performance on convolution- and transformer-based vision tasks.

## Conclusion (from the paper)

We developed a principled approach for quantization-aware training (QAT) based on a framework of convex, piecewise-affine regularization (PAR). In order to avoid the diminishing regularization effect of the standard proximal SGD method, we derive an aggregate proximal (AProx) algorithm and prove its last-iterate convergence. Our experiments demonstrate that PARQ achieves competitive performance compared with QAT methods that correspond to using nonconvex regularization. Compared with using hard-quantization (STE) throughout the training process, the gradual evolution of PARQ from piecewise-affine soft quantization to hard quantization helps the training process to be more stable, and often converges to better local minima. This is more evident in the most demanding cases of low-bit quantization of smaller models.
