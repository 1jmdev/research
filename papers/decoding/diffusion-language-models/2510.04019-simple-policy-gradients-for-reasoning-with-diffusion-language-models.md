## Abstract

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) represent a promising alternative to autoregressive LLMs; however, the lack of effective post-training techniques, including reinforcement learning (RL), remains a key challenge for dLLMs, especially for downstream applications. Existing approaches often rely on a sequence-level view that requires biased likelihood approximations. In this work, we propose Amortized Group Relative Policy Optimization (AGRPO), a policy gradient algorithm that leverages the Markovian nature of dLLMs, optimizing individual denoising steps rather than full sequences. Our approach improves alignment between the trained policy and the inference process and also admits efficient, unbiased gradient updates via a novel timestep estimation scheme. We demonstrate AGRPO's effectiveness on different math and reasoning tasks, achieving absolute accuracy gains of +59.4\% and +69.7\% on Countdown and Sudoku over the base LLaDA model, exceeding comparable methods such as diffu-GRPO. Code is available at https://github.com/probablyabot/agrpo.

## Contributions (from the paper)

Our main contributions are as follows: Soundness. We derive a policy gradient framework for dLLMs grounded in the denoising MDP and explain how the stepwise objective sidesteps the need for ELBO-like approximations. Efficiency. Through a new timestep estimation technique, we show how to implement our algorithm in a compute-efficient way that avoids costly full-trajectory updates. We analyze the tradeoff between sampling budget and reward, and propose two variance reduction techniques for the reasoning setting. Efficacy. We train models on four reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH, Countdown, and Sudoku), showing that AGRPO consistently outperforms previous ELBO-based methods. Furthermore, models trained with AGRPO retain high accuracy even when sampled with much fewer steps, a unique capability of post-trained dLLMs.

## Conclusion (from the paper)

This work presents AGRPO, a policy gradient algorithm designed for dLLMs, grounded in the multi-step denoising perspective of RL. Unlike previous works that rely on sequence likelihood bounds, AGRPO uses tractable stepwise likelihoods, allowing for unbiased policy updates and better train/inference alignment. A key contribution is showing how to compute these updates in a practical, scalable way via timestep estimation and novel variance reduction techniques. Using our proposed algorithm, we post-train dLLMs on multiple reasoning tasks, beating comparable RL methods and redefining the inference compute/quality frontier. Our results demonstrate that policy gradient RL techniques can be effectively extended to the dLLM setting without the need for biased sequence-likelihood approximations; we hope future works can build on our methods, either theoretically or empirically, and further close the gap between dLLM and AR LLM post-training.
